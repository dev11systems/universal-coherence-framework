# Universal Coherence Framework — Model v1

## Overview
The UCF model describes how systems maintain identity, stability, and adaptability across time.  
It focuses on the patterns that appear in biological, psychological, technological, social, and ecological domains.

Coherence is treated as a structural property:  
the degree to which a system’s internal states, external behaviors, and temporal trajectory reinforce each other.

This document outlines the first version of the model.

---

## 1. Coherence Principle
A system remains coherent when its:

- internal processes do not contradict its own functioning  
- interactions with other systems remain intelligible and stable  
- structure maintains integrity under change  
- trajectory across time forms a continuous arc rather than fragmentation  

Coherence is not static; it is maintained through ongoing adjustment.

---

## 2. Four Layers of Coherence

### Internal
How a system organizes itself.  
Patterns: integration, prediction, memory, constraint, alignment.

### Inter-agent
How systems synchronize and coordinate.  
Patterns: communication, signaling, protocol, trust, mutual modeling.

### Systemic
How larger structures maintain stability.  
Patterns: flows, roles, redundancy, governance, topology.

### Temporal
How coherence persists through time.  
Patterns: narrative, continuity, adaptation, revision, evolution.

---

## 3. Cross-Domain Matrix

| Domain | Internal | Inter-agent | Systemic | Temporal |
|-------|----------|-------------|----------|----------|
| Biological | homeostasis | signaling | ecology | evolution |
| Psychological | identity | relationships | culture | narrative |
| Technological | state integrity | APIs/protocols | architecture | versioning |
| Social | values | norms | institutions | history |
| Ecological | species traits | interspecies dynamics | ecosystem | succession |

The matrix is used to identify shared structures across domains.

---

## 4. Coherence Dynamics

### Stabilizing feedback  
Restores equilibrium.

### Amplifying feedback  
Drives growth, learning, and adaptation.

### Integrative feedback  
Combines subsystems into higher-order coherence.

### Dissipative feedback  
Degrades structure or pushes the system toward fragmentation.

Coherence is the balance among these forces.

---

## 5. Coherence Stressors
Conditions that disrupt coherence:

- overload  
- noise  
- ambiguity  
- internal contradiction  
- environmental instability  
- incentive mismatch  
- information fragmentation  

Stressors reveal the system’s coherence limits.

---

## 6. Coherence Capacities (v1)
Ways to assess a system’s coherence:

- integration capacity  
- stability capacity  
- adaptation capacity  
- alignment capacity  
- temporal continuity capacity  

These are domain-agnostic and comparable across systems.
