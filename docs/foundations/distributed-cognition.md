# Predictive Processing and Coherence

**(Foundations Document · Evidence-Based Concepts)**

This document outlines the predictive-processing foundations relevant to the Universal Coherence Framework (UCF). Predictive processing is one of the strongest scientific models explaining how living and artificial systems maintain coherence, regulate internal states, and respond adaptively to uncertainty.

This document is strictly evidence-based and does not include speculative extensions.

## 1. Overview of Predictive Processing

### **Definition**

Predictive processing (PP) is a framework in cognitive and computational neuroscience that describes the brain as a prediction engine. Systems generate models of the world and continuously update them to minimize **prediction error** or **surprise**.

### **Core Principles**

* Systems predict incoming signals before they arrive.
* Incoming sensory or environmental data is compared against predictions.
* Mismatches (prediction errors) drive updates to internal models.
* The goal is to maintain a stable, low-surprise internal state.

### **Relevance to Coherence**

Coherence emerges when:

* predictions match incoming data,
* errors are minimized,
* internal and external states become aligned.

This corresponds directly to UCF’s Flow state.

## 2. The Free Energy Principle (FEP)

### **Definition**

The Free Energy Principle (Friston) proposes that adaptive systems resist disorder by minimizing variational free energy—a measurable proxy for surprise or prediction error.

### **Key Insights**

* Organisms maintain coherence by reducing uncertainty.
* Perception, action, and learning all serve to minimize free energy.
* Systems that fail to minimize free energy drift toward chaos or collapse.

### **Why It Matters for UCF**

FEP provides an empirical and mathematical grounding for:

* transitions between coherence states,
* the role of uncertainty in destabilizing systems,
* the mechanisms that push agents from Chaos → Tension → Flow.

## 3. Hierarchical Predictive Models

### **Definition**

Predictive systems use hierarchical models where higher levels encode abstract predictions and lower levels process detailed sensory input.

### **Evidence Basis**

Brain imaging and computational modeling show:

* structured, layered prediction error flows,
* top-down predictions interacting with bottom-up signals,
* coherence depending on stable synchronization across levels.

### **Connection to Coherence**

Breakdown at any level leads to:

* noise,
* conflict,
* instability,
* degraded internal coherence.

This corresponds to UCF’s Tension or Chaos states.

## 4. Precision Weighting and Uncertainty

### **Definition**

Systems assign **precision** (confidence) to predictions or sensory data.

### **Evidence Basis**

* High precision on predictions → stability but risk of rigidity.
* High precision on sensory data → flexibility but risk of overwhelm.
* Dysfunctional precision weighting is implicated in disorders (e.g., anxiety, psychosis).

### **Why It Matters for UCF**

Precision weighting helps explain:

* why systems diverge under stress,
* what causes coherence loss,
* how stability can be restored (via rebalancing precision).

This supports practical interpretations of Flow and Unity.

## 5. Action as Prediction Error Minimization

### **Definition**

Under predictive processing, action is not merely reaction—it is *active inference*: systems act to change the world so that predictions come true.

### **Evidence Basis**

Examples:

* Eye movements seek predicted sensory input.
* Organisms move away from surprising states.
* Machine learning systems adjust weights to reduce loss (error).

### **Why It Matters for UCF**

Coherence is achieved through the interplay of:

* updating internal models (learning),
* changing external conditions (action),
* regulating precision (confidence),
* stabilizing feedback loops.

These mechanisms align strongly with UCF’s state transition logic.
